# Changelog

All notable changes to liton are documented in this file. The format is based on [Keep a Changelog](https://keepachangelog.com/en/1.1.0/), and liton follows [Semantic Versioning](https://semver.org/) (before 1.0, a minor or patch release may still break things).

## [Unreleased]

### Added

- `zig build run` serves liton at http://127.0.0.1:8095 (`LITON_HOST` and `LITON_PORT` change it): panes, each a widget bound to a capability the daemon advertises. Add panes from Settings; a widget whose capability no provider supplies shows what it needs.
- Sessions, Chat and Capabilities widgets on chat.v1. Chat panes follow the session selected in Sessions, or are pinned to one; they show the agent's messages, tool calls and results, and ask you to allow or deny what it requests.
- Settings, behind the toolbar's one button, picks the Developer or Tiling preset, adds widgets (marking those no provider supports yet) and shows the layout as a snapshot, JSON to copy or to paste and load; the browser keeps the last layout. The working UI shows no buttons for adding widgets.
- The Developer preset, the default, is a workbench like Paseo's: Sessions in a left drawer, the chat in the middle, and Changes (once a provider supplies `workspace.git`) and Capabilities as tabs in a right drawer. The ◧ and ◨ buttons in the toolbar hide and show the drawers. On a phone the three are full-width panels you swipe between, opening on the chat; the strip above them shows which is in view and jumps to one when tapped.
- A mock provider of scripted agents, so all of this works without a real backend.
- The Tiling preset tiles panes in columns. Move a pane into the column beside it with its ← and → buttons (past the edge it gets a column of its own), switch a column between a stack and tabs, and make it narrower or wider. It gives the chat a column twice as wide.
- In Tiling on a phone, a strip of tabs above the panes picks the one column shown.
- Sessions waiting for you to allow or deny something stand out: Sessions shows each status as a badge, and a chat pane showing such a session says "waiting for you" in its header, next to the session's title.
- A tool call shows as one card with its result: the tool and whether it is running, done or failed, its input, and its output, with all but the first 4 lines folded away until you open them.
- Connect a running Paseo daemon by setting `PASEO_PASSWORD` (and `PASEO_HOST` or `PASEO_PORT` if it isn't at 127.0.0.1:6767): its agents show up as sessions, and you can chat with them and allow or deny what they ask. `LITON_MOCK=off` hides the scripted mock agents.
- An Inbox widget lists every session by what it needs from you: Needs you, then Failed, Done and Working, each with its agent, state and the agent's last message. Click one to open it in the chat (on a phone, it jumps to the chat). Allow or deny what a session asks for right in the inbox.
- Settle an inbox item, or snooze it for an hour, to move it to Later until something new happens in that session or the hour is up. It moves at once, and comes back with why if the daemon refuses.
- The Inbox preset, in Settings: a workbench with the Inbox in the left drawer, the chat in the middle, and the right drawer hidden. Paseo agents that finished since you last looked show as done.
- The browser tab's title counts the sessions that need you, as in "(2) liton", and so does the Inbox's tab.
- Settings is now a sidebar that slides over the panes from the right (the whole screen on a phone). Besides the preset, it lists each drawer's or column's panes: move a pane to another drawer it fits, remove it, or open it to set its options. Adding a widget offers only those that fit. Every change shows at once.
- Per-pane options, set in Settings: a chat shows tool output in full, folded or not at all, and shows or hides status lines; Sessions shows or hides each session's agent; the Inbox can list idle sessions too, and snoozes for an hour, a day or a week (its Snooze button says which).
- The right drawer's Changes shows the focused session's branch, how far it is ahead of and behind its base, and its changed files, uncommitted or against the base; click a file to see its diff, unified or split (an option in Settings), and open the branch's commits to see each one's files. It works with Paseo or any provider of `workspace.git`, and asks for updates only while it is on screen.
- Connect a Paseo daemon reached through a relay with pairing, end-to-end encrypted: run `bridges/paseo-relay/bridge.mjs` with the pairing offer and the password, and point `PASEO_HOST` and `PASEO_PORT` at it. It keeps one connection to the daemon however many liton daemons use it.
- The daemon offers its capabilities through one documented protocol at `/liton/v1` (docs/protocol.md): `GET /liton/v1/manifest` lists its providers, transformers and topics, `POST /liton/v1/call` calls chat.v1, attention.v1 and workspaces.v1 with one set of error codes, and `POST /liton/v1/poll` follows sessions, timelines, the inbox, workspaces and git changes at once, each from a cursor.
- `/liton/v1/poll` follows the hunks of a changed file (`workspace.git.v1`'s `file/<context>/<scope>/<path>` topic) and the sessions Paseo's providers keep that Paseo doesn't run (`chat.v1`'s `importable` topic).
- The `liton` command line (`zig build cli`) speaks that protocol and prints JSON lines: `liton manifest`, `liton call chat.v1 '"ListSessions"'` (the request also from stdin; exits 1 on an error), and `liton watch chat.v1 timeline/<session>`, one event per line until interrupted, for piping into `jq` and other tools. It finds the daemon at `LITON_URL` (default http://127.0.0.1:8095).
- Add providers outside the daemon, written in any language, or other liton daemons, with `LITON_PROVIDERS="name=http://host:port,…"`: each serves the same `/liton/v1` protocol, and its sessions, workspaces and timelines show up beside your own, named `<name>/…`, and take your messages. The daemon asks each at most once a second (`LITON_PROVIDERS_INTERVAL_MS`) however many clients poll, and one that is down leaves out only its own sessions until it answers again. `examples/provider-echo/` is the smallest such provider.
- Describe a setup in a `Litonfile.roc`, a Roc program: the providers outside the daemon to connect, the transformers it runs and the presets Settings offers. It builds on liton's own, or on another setup it imports, and changes what it needs (`examples/litonfile-team/`). `liton setup [path]` evaluates it to `liton.setup.json`, the same file every time.
- The daemon starts from `liton.setup.json`, or the setup `LITON_SETUP` names: its remotes and transformers, with `LITON_PROVIDERS` and `LITON_TRANSFORMERS` added over them, and its presets in the manifest.
- Settings offers the presets of the daemon's setup, and a browser with no layout of its own starts with the setup's default. Change it from there, and the browser keeps your layout. A preset shows as picked while the layout is still its own.
- A new look: each pane is a glass tile with rounded corners, evenly spaced over a dark gradient. The pane you last clicked in has a bright edge and the others dim a little. Panes and Settings fade in when they open, and buttons respond as you hover and press them. All motion stops when your system asks for reduced motion.
- A bar across the top replaces the toolbar. On the left are your setup's presets as numbered workspaces, with the one shown marked: click one, or press Alt+1 to Alt+9 outside a text box. On the right are how many sessions need you, how many agents are running, the drawer buttons and Settings. Escape closes Settings.
- Themes: Pastoral, gold over Thomas Cole's painting "The Arcadian or Pastoral State", dimmed under dark glass (the default); Hypr, with an edge that fades from teal to violet; and Plain, light and calm with nothing moving. Pick one in Settings. The browser keeps it with your layout, and a snapshot carries it. A setup declares its themes and its default one (`Setup.with_theme`, `with_default_theme`), and Settings offers the daemon's. A theme's background is a gradient, a color, or an image at a URL, dimmed (`Image({ url, dim })`), such as those the daemon serves from `ui/backgrounds/`.
- A Glass switch in Settings turns off the blur behind the tiles, which saves work on a slow device's graphics card.
- Choose what shows behind the tiles in Settings → Background: the theme's own, an image of the setup's themes, such as Pastoral's painting, or any image's URL, and how far a slider dims it. The browser keeps your choice, apart from your layout.
- Stop a running agent with chat.v1's `Cancel` call (`liton call chat.v1 '{"Cancel":{"session_id":"…"}}'`), for Paseo's agents, the mock's and those of providers outside the daemon.
- Each chat.v1 session tells, when its provider knows, the model it runs, the directory and workspace it works in, its branch, and when its running turn started and it last changed, for an agent's header to show.
- Browse the files of a session's or workspace's directory with the new `files.v1` capability: `/liton/v1/poll` follows a directory's entries (`tree/<context>/<path>`) and a file's text, up to its first 256 KiB, or whether it is binary or too large (`file/<context>/<path>`). Paseo, the mock and providers outside the daemon offer it; the daemon asks Paseo for a directory or file when it is first watched and again at most every 10 s while it is.
- A chat.v1 permission request carries its body as Markdown, such as the plan an agent asks you to approve, and the provider's own choices, such as Paseo's Reject, Implement and Implement with Auto; `AnswerPermission` takes one of them by its `choice`. The mock agent asks for a plan when your message mentions one.
- A chat.v1 timeline marks where each turn ends, with when it started and ended (`TurnEnded`), and a session's `running_since` holds while its agent waits for you mid-turn, for a working timer and a "worked for" line.
- Rename and archive sessions (chat.v1's `Rename` and `Archive`), rename, pin and archive workspaces, and rename projects (workspaces.v1's `Rename`, `Pin`, `Archive` and `RenameProject`), for Paseo's, the mock's and those of providers outside the daemon. A session is pinned by pinning its workspace, as in Paseo.
- The daemon transcribes dictation on your own machine: set `LITON_STT=sherpa=ws://127.0.0.1:6006` (Parakeet in sherpa-onnx) or `LITON_STT=whisper=http://127.0.0.1:8178` (whisper.cpp), which `rig/speech/` runs, and it offers `voice.v1`. Clients post the audio in acknowledged 1 s chunks to `/liton/v1/audio/voice.v1/<utterance>/<seq>`, so a dropped link loses nothing, and `Finish` answers the transcript (docs/protocol.md, Audio).
- Dictate into a chat's composer: with the daemon offering `voice.v1`, a mic button beside Send listens at once, showing your voice's level. Tap it to start and again to stop, or hold it while you talk. The transcript goes in where the cursor was, for you to edit before sending. The browser asks for the microphone on the first press; the button says when it is blocked, or when the speech engine is down. Its code loads only on that first press.
- Talk with an agent hands-free: the conversation button beside the mic listens continuously, sends each thing you say once you pause, and speaks the agent's replies sentence by sentence, naming code blocks and long lists rather than reading them. A tap on the mic stops a reply being spoken. Settings picks the voice, those on your device first, and how fast it speaks.
- Messages in the chat show as Markdown: headings, bold, italic and struck-through text, inline code, lists (nested too), quotes and links, which open in a new tab; a table shows as its text, in a code block. Code blocks are highlighted for Roc, shell, JSON, TypeScript and JavaScript, Rust, Zig, Go and C-like languages, Python, Nix, YAML and diffs, and have a Copy button that says "Copied" as you press it. Only links to the web or to email addresses work, and any HTML in a message shows as text.
- A chat pane's header shows the agent it shows: the session's title (in full on hover), a dot and word for its status (pulsing while running, marked while waiting for you, red when it failed), the agent and its model, the workspace's folder and branch, and a Stop button while it works. Stop shows "Stopping…" at once, and if the agent can't be stopped, says why and offers Stop again.
- "Jump to latest" appears over a chat scrolled up from its newest message, in browsers with scroll-driven CSS animations, such as Chrome and Edge.
- Start an agent with chat.v1's `Create`, in a workspace, of a kind its provider offers (chat.v1's new `agents` topic lists them), with a model and a first prompt; and make a workspace of a project, a checkout of it or a worktree on a new branch, with workspaces.v1's `Create` (docs/protocol.md). Each answers the new session's or workspace's id. The mock provider and providers outside the daemon take both.
- Start Paseo's agents and create its workspaces through those calls too, an agent also in any directory of Paseo's.
- A Workspaces widget lists your projects, each with its workspaces and their sessions: a dot for what each needs (a workspace's is its most urgent session's), its branch, and its uncommitted changes as "+3 −1" when the provider or a Changes pane has counted them. Fold a project away, which the browser remembers, or group the workspaces by what they need from you, and hide idle ones, in its options. Click a workspace to show it: the chat shows its session that needs you most, and Changes shows the workspace's changes. Click a session under it to show that one. Sessions now lists its sessions the same way, a row each with its dot.
- Start an agent from Workspaces: + on a workspace's row opens a small form with the kinds of agent its provider offers, a model if you want one and a first prompt, and + on a project, or in the pane's header, creates a workspace of it, a worktree on a new branch or a checkout. Each shows at once as starting, in the tree and in the chat, until the daemon lists it; one the provider refuses goes away again, and the form comes back with why.
- On a phone, back returns from a chat you opened in Workspaces to the list, and forward goes to the chat again. The page's address names the panel shown (`#left`, `#main` or `#right`), so reloading keeps it.
- Review an agent's work with the new `review.v1` capability, which the daemon hosts for every session and workspace: comment on lines of a file or of a diff, and on the whole review, as drafts, then `Send` them to the agent as one Markdown message, each comment under the file and lines it is about with those lines quoted. Sent comments are marked with their round, and threads stay until you resolve them. A thread follows its lines when the file changes, and is marked outdated once they are gone (docs/protocol.md, Review).
- A Files widget, beside Changes in the right drawer, browses the files of the focused session's or workspace's directory: open a folder to list it, and pick a file to see its lines, numbered and highlighted, or why it can't show them (binary, too large, unreadable, or only its first part). It works with any provider of `files.v1`, and asks for updates only while it is on screen.
- Comment on code in Files and in Changes: click a line's number, and then another's for the lines between, to write a comment under them in Markdown, which shows at once as a draft beside its lines, marked in the gutter. Reply to a thread, edit or delete a draft, and resolve a thread, which folds it. Open in Files, over a diff in Changes, shows the whole file.
- Send a review from Files or Changes: each pane's Review counts the drafts and the rounds sent, lists the threads, each opening a reply under its lines, takes a comment on the whole review, and Send review sends the drafts to the agent as one message. They show as sent at once, and as drafts again, with why, if the daemon can't send them.
- attention.v1 lists what needs you first and then the latest, and its items tell when their session last changed (`updated`) and whether it changed since it was last seen (`unread`); its new `Seen` call marks sessions seen, or unread again (docs/protocol.md).
- Unread, as in email: a session that finished, failed or asks for you since you last looked has a dot and a bolder title in the Inbox, and a ring round its dot in Workspaces and Sessions. Opening it, or having it in a chat on screen, reads it; Mark read, Mark unread and Mark all read in the Inbox do it by hand. Each shows at once.
- The Chat preset, in Settings: your sessions, the latest first, beside one large chat, with the right drawer hidden.
- Each project, workspace and session in Workspaces and Sessions has a ⋮ menu, which a right-click opens too: a new workspace of a project, a new agent in a workspace or beside a session, and for a session, Stop agent while it works, Mark read or unread, and Copy id.
- Keys, as in Paseo: Ctrl+B or ⌘B and Ctrl+E or ⌘E show or hide the left and right drawers, and Ctrl+, or ⌘, opens or closes Settings, with ⌘ in the message box too, where Ctrl+B and Ctrl+E still move the cursor on a Mac. Escape stops the agent of the chat you are in, once there is no menu, Settings or new agent or workspace form left for it to close first. The drawer buttons and Settings name their keys when you hover them.
- While an agent works on a turn, waiting for you too, its chat shows a loader at the bottom and for how long, as "3m 46s", counting each second.
- Where a turn ends, the chat says how long the agent worked on it, as "Worked for 2m 14s", when its provider tells when the turn started.
- A permission request shows as a card: what the agent asks, the plan or other text its provider sends with it, as Markdown, and the provider's own choices as buttons, such as Reject, Implement and Implement with Auto for a plan, or else Allow and Deny. In the chat and in the Inbox alike, a choice hides the card at once, and a refused one brings it back with why.
- Pin a workspace from its ⋮ menu, or a session's, which pins its workspace, as in Paseo: it lists first in its project, marked with a pin, until you unpin it. Archive a workspace or a session from its ⋮ menu, which removes it from the list. Each shows at once, and is undone with why if the provider refuses it.
- Rename a project, a workspace or a session from its ⋮ menu: its title becomes a field, Enter saves the new name, which shows at once, and Escape leaves it as it was. A rename the provider refuses is undone, with why.
- The protocol has `rooms.v1`, rooms where people and agents talk: channels, forums, direct conversations and announcements, with their members, messages, threads and reactions, from any NIP-29 relay or other source of rooms. List rooms, send a message or a reply, react, and join or leave a room with `liton call rooms.v1`, and follow a room with `liton watch rooms.v1 messages/<room>`.
- The mock provider offers `rooms.v1`: three rooms, #general, the forum #ideas and the announcements of #releases, where you talk with Ada and the agents Scout and Patch. An agent a message mentions types and then answers in the message's thread.
- A Rooms widget, in Settings' Add a widget, lists the rooms `rooms.v1` offers, each with its kind and what is unread in it. Join a room or leave one, shown at once and undone with why if the provider refuses.
- Open a room from Rooms to read it: each message's author, marked if an agent, how long ago it was posted, its text as Markdown, whether it was edited or deleted, its reactions and its thread's replies, and who is typing. A forum shows its posts as cards.
- Open a message's thread from its replies in a room: its replies and who is typing in it show beside the room, or in its place in a narrow pane.
- Write in a room, or reply in a thread, from a composer like the chat's. A message shows at once as sending until the room posts it; one the provider refuses, such as in a room you haven't joined or one only others announce in, stays with why, to retry or discard.
- Mention a room's members as you write: typing @ offers the others in the room, narrowing as you type their name, and a message names those it @mentions, so an agent you mention answers in its thread.
- React to a room's messages, or take your reaction back, from the reactions under each: it counts at once, and comes back with why if the provider refuses.
- Drag a pane by its tab or header with the mouse to move it: in the Developer, Inbox and Chat presets to another drawer or the main area its widget fits, or to another place among a drawer's tabs; in Tiling into another column or its tabs, before or after a stacked pane, or past a pane's outer edge into a new column. The column it would drop into is outlined and a bar marks where it lands; Escape or letting go outside the columns leaves it where it was. On a touch screen, panes still move from their menu.
- `tickets.v1`: issues and tickets from your trackers, listed with filters, read with their comments, commented on, and moved between statuses and assignees, each with a link back to where it lives. A session links to the ticket its branch names, unless you link or unlink it by hand. The mock provider offers scripted tickets of a GitHub-like and a Linear-like source to try it with, through `liton call tickets.v1` (docs/protocol.md, Tickets).

- Change a ticket from its Tickets pane: set its status to one of its source's, assign it to its source's people or take them off, and comment on it in Markdown. Each change shows at once, marked Saving… until the daemon has it, and goes back, with why, if the tracker refuses it.
- A ticket shows the sessions working on it, linked by hand or by their branch: open one to focus it, as the chat shows it, or unlink it. Link to links the session in focus to the ticket. Each shows at once, and goes back, with why, if the daemon refuses it.

Type to filter it, pick with the arrows or Ctrl+N and Ctrl+P, and run with Enter or a click; what you ran lists first next time, and Escape or Ctrl+K again closes it.

- On a Mac, the palette, the list of keys, Settings and the buttons' tooltips write keys as ⌘B and ⌥1; elsewhere as Ctrl+B and Alt+1.

### Changed

- liton's development environment has one Roc compiler, nightly-2026-11-15, for the daemon and the UI alike: `roc-ui` is gone, and `zig build`'s `-Droc-ui` defaults to the `-Droc` compiler. A release build of the daemon takes minutes rather than about twenty.
- **Breaking:** the daemon refuses a request from a page of another site, so a website open in your browser can't send your agents work: one whose `Origin` isn't the daemon's own address, a POST not sent as `application/json`, and one to a host name other than `LITON_HOST`, `localhost` or one `LITON_ALLOWED_HOSTS` lists. Behind a proxy or a domain of its own, set `LITON_ALLOWED_HOSTS` to its name, such as `LITON_ALLOWED_HOSTS=liton.example`. A client of the protocol sends its calls and polls with `Content-Type: application/json`.
- **Breaking:** the Paseo and speech rigs run from liton's development tool rather than shell scripts: `zig build devtool -- paseo daemon` (and `paseo cli`, `paseo relay`, `paseo bridge`) in place of `rig/paseo/*.sh`, and `zig build devtool -- speech sherpa` (and `speech whisper`, `speech model`) in place of `rig/speech/*.sh`, with the same settings.
- Sending a message or allowing or denying a request shows at once: the message appears as "Sending…" and the prompt gives way to "Allowing…" or "Denying…" until the agent has it. A message the agent did not take stays, with why and a Retry button; an answer it did not take brings the prompt back. A chat pane says "Loading…" until its session's timeline arrives.
- The browser remembers which drawers you hid, and each pane's options. Layouts saved before options existed load with every option at its default.
- Paseo works over its WebSocket alone, with Paseo 0.9 through 0.15: agents of every provider show up, not only Claude, Codex and OpenCode, and a password Paseo refuses shows as a failed "Paseo refused the password in PASEO_PASSWORD" session, retried every minute.
- Layouts saved by an earlier version, in the browser or as a snapshot, load in Tiling, the oldest with one column per pane. One that is just the old default of Sessions, Chat and Capabilities loads as the new Developer workbench instead.
- Switching sessions, picking a file or a scope in Changes, or showing a pane brings what it shows faster: the UI asks the daemon at once instead of on its next once-a-second update, which now asks for everything on screen in one request.
- Without an Inbox in the layout, the browser tab's title counts the sessions waiting for you.
- Layouts saved before themes existed show the setup's default theme. A `liton.setup.json` evaluated before then still loads, and offers no themes of its own.
- Each pane's header shows what the pane shows. What it follows or is pinned to, Pin, the moves in Tiling and Close pane are in its ⋯ menu.
- The Developer and Inbox presets' right drawer holds Changes and Files, as tabs. Capabilities is still in Tiling, and Settings → Add a widget adds it.
- The Developer preset's left drawer is Workspaces, the sessions under their workspaces. Sessions is still in Tiling, and Settings → Add a widget adds it.
- On a phone, the Workbench's panels sit over a glass tab bar at the bottom, Workspaces, Chat and Changes, each with its icon, in place of the strip of dots above them. The Workspaces tab counts what needs you, and the tab bar follows your swipes.
- The message box grows to a few lines as you type: Enter sends and Shift+Enter starts a new line, while on a touch screen Enter starts one and Send sends. On a phone it is round, with Send beside it, and the tab bar gives way to the keyboard; a chat's header is its title, status dot and Stop over a smaller line of the agent and where it works.
- On a phone, Settings slides up from the bottom as a sheet over a dimmed screen, which a tap closes, and the top bar holds only liton, what needs you and Settings: pick a preset in Settings. Rows, buttons and menus are at least 44 pixels tall, and a row's + shows without hovering.
- A diff in Changes highlights each line's code, as a chat's code blocks do.
- Send, or Enter, while dictating stops the recording and sends what you typed with what you said after it, in one go. Tapping the mic still stops it to edit before sending. If nothing is transcribed, nothing is sent: what you typed stays, with why.
- The Inbox reads like an email inbox: what needs you on top, then every other session in one list, the latest first, each with its state and the agent's last message, rather than a section for each state. It lists idle sessions too, unless you turn that option off.
- Workspaces and Sessions list the latest first: sessions by when they last changed, workspaces by their latest session and projects by their latest workspace. Their new Order option lists them by name instead. A browser opens on the latest session.
- The presets are no longer in the top bar: pick one in Settings, or press Alt+1 to Alt+9.
- In a conversation, liton no longer hears its own replies from your speakers as you talking: it stops listening while it speaks a reply and for a moment after, and drops anything it hears that is mostly the last reply read back, saying so. Tap the mic, which shows a stop while it speaks, to stop speaking and listen at once; the agent keeps working, and Stop beside the message box stops it.
- A selected row, in Workspaces, Sessions, the Inbox or Changes, shows as a lighter surface rather than a gold tint and bar.
- Status has colours of its own in every theme: blue for running, green for done, amber for what needs you and red for failed, and a diff's added and removed lines are green and red. The theme's accent, gold in Pastoral, is left for focus and the main action.
- A workspace's uncommitted changes show their lines added in green and removed in red, and nothing until they are counted, rather than "changed".
- A session's or workspace's status, in a row or a chat's header, is an icon rather than a word: a blue ring turning while it runs, an amber alert when it needs you, a red triangle when it failed, a green dot when it is done and unread, a faint one once read, and nothing while idle. Hover it for the word, which screen readers read. Unread sessions no longer have a gold ring, only a bolder title.
- Settings, the drawer buttons, a pane's menu and + are icons rather than words and glyphs, with the same names for screen readers, which a pointer resting on a pane's menu or + also shows.
- Send and Stop are one round button at the end of the message box: an arrow that sends, which turns into a red Stop while the agent works and nothing is typed, and a turning ring once pressed until the agent stops. Stop is no longer in a chat's header.
- A tool call is one muted line, its tool and what it ran, that opens on its input and output. It shimmers while it runs and turns red if it failed.
- Tool calls one after another fold into one line, how many and which tools, that opens on them.
- A chat pane's new Collapse option, in Settings, shows every tool call open (Full), folds tool calls in a row into one line (Tools, the default), or also folds each finished turn's work into one line between your message and the agent's last reply (Turns). A turn ends where its provider marks its end, or else where your next message starts, or once the agent stops.
- Workspaces lists one row per session, which says how long ago it last changed ("now", "3m", "2h", "4d"). A workspace with one session is that session's row, with its branch if it is a worktree and its changes, and its + starts another agent beside it; a workspace with several still has a row of its own over them. Projects are written in sentence case rather than capitals.
- The Developer, Inbox and Chat presets have no top bar. The drawer buttons are at the two ends of the chat's header, Settings is a gear at the bottom left of the left drawer (or in the chat's header while that drawer is hidden), and how many sessions need you shows in the Workspaces header as well as the browser tab's title. On a phone the bar with liton's name is gone too, and the gear is in the Workspaces header. What went wrong shows as a toast at the bottom until you dismiss it. Tiling keeps its bar.
- The right drawer's button, at the end of the chat's header, shows the lines the chat's workspace has added and removed but not committed, in green and red, so you can tell there is something to review without opening the drawer. Hovering a drawer's button names what the drawer holds, such as Changes · Files.
- A chat reads as a centred column at most 820px wide, in 15px text, with your own messages in a neutral grey bubble rather than a gold one.
- The message box is one rounded box under the chat's column: what you type on top, and a row below it with a chip naming the session's model (or its agent, when the provider doesn't say which model), the mic and conversation buttons, and Send.
- Changes picks its scope, uncommitted or against the base, from a drop-down rather than two buttons, and shows the lines its files add and remove in green and red beside it.
- The review over Changes and Files shows only while it has drafts or threads still open, rather than always saying "no drafts", and Send review only while there are drafts to send.
- Changes lists its files as a tree of folders, each with the lines its files add and remove, which fold away when clicked; a coloured square says whether a file was added, modified or deleted.
- A file's diff in Changes opens in the main area, in place of the chat, with Open in main area; files picked then show there too, until Back to Chat.
- In the Developer, Inbox and Chat presets a pane's menu hides the pane rather than closing it, or hides its drawer when it is the last pane the drawer shows; Show in the menu of a pane beside it brings it back. Hidden panes stay hidden after a reload. Settings' Remove still closes a pane, and Tiling's menus still offer Close pane.
- Tabs show their widget's icon beside its title, and a × on hover that hides the tab in the Developer, Inbox and Chat presets and closes it in Tiling.
- In Tiling, a pane's menu switches its column between a stack and tabs, in place of the column's Stack and Tabs buttons, and offers it only when the column holds more than one pane.
- A pane's menu names where it moves the pane: "Move to right drawer" or "Move to main area" in the Developer, Inbox and Chat presets, for each place its widget fits, and "Move to the column on the left" or "Move to a new column on the right" in Tiling, rather than ← and → arrows.
- The Inbox preset has Rooms as a tab beside the Inbox, in the left drawer.
- A room picked in Rooms now shows in a Room pane, its own widget, rather than in place of the list: the list stays beside it, marking the room shown. The Inbox preset has a Room tab beside the chat, which picking a room brings forward, as picking a session brings the chat back. Pin a Room pane from its menu to keep it on one room. A saved layout's Rooms pane gets a Room pane beside it.
- A ticket picked in Tickets now shows in a Ticket pane, its own widget, rather than in place of the list: the list keeps its filters and marks the ticket shown. The Developer preset has a Ticket tab beside the chat, hidden until you pick a ticket, which brings it forward, as picking a session brings the chat back. Pin a Ticket pane from its menu to keep it on one ticket. A saved layout's Tickets pane gets a Ticket pane beside it.
- Layouts are a grid of tiles rather than columns: a tile holds one or more panes as tabs, and spans rows and columns of the grid. Drop a pane on the edge of a tile to open a new tile on that side of it (in the Developer, Inbox and Chat presets, above or below a drawer's, or on any side within the main area), or on its middle to add it to the tile's tabs. In Tiling a pane's menu moves it to the tile on the left, the right, above or below, or past the edge into a new tile, and − and + make a tile narrower or wider. A saved layout of columns loads as the same grid, a stacked column as a tile for each pane.
- Dragging a pane by its tab or header shows the layout as if you dropped it where the pointer is, the other tiles making room, with its title following the pointer; the tiles slide to their new places where the browser can animate it, unless motion is reduced. Escape, or letting go outside the tiles, puts everything back.
- Keys for the layout, also in the ⌘K palette and the `?` list: outside a text box, Ctrl+Shift or ⌘⇧ with an arrow focuses the tile that way, as does W then H, J, K or L; W then M, X, T or R and one of those letters moves the pane, swaps the tiles, joins the tile into the other's tabs or moves the tile's edge that way; W then V or B splits the pane off into a new tile to the right or below.
- Drag the line between two tiles to resize the columns or rows beside it; in the Developer, Inbox and Chat presets the drawers keep their width, and the main area's tiles and rows resize.
- Workspaces grouped by status list them in Paseo's order, each group headed by its icon: Needs input (an agent waits for you or failed), Ready to review (done, with something you haven't seen, and changes), Working and Done.
- The Developer preset takes Paseo's shape: Workspaces lists the workspaces by status, each once, and the main area shows the one picked as tabs of its sessions, starting on the latest session's workspace. A workspace is as urgent as its most urgent session. Set Workspaces' Group by and Sessions options in Settings to list them under their projects with their sessions as rows, as before.

### Fixed

- Password managers such as Bitwarden, 1Password and LastPass no longer offer to fill the ⌘K palette's search box.
- On a phone, a tool call that is still running shows its card again, not an empty box.
- The page title's count of what needs you now leaves out sessions you settled or snoozed, and is the same whatever the layout, not only when an Inbox pane is open.
- Sending a message by pressing Enter clears the message box, so the next message no longer starts with the text of the last.
- A message with a blank line inside a code block no longer freezes the page.
- Clicks, keys and scrolling no longer lag while a long session is open: a chat shows the latest 50 entries of its timeline, and earlier ones as you scroll up to them (or press Show earlier). Jump to latest shows only the latest again.
- On a phone, scrolling and taps no longer stutter: panes there no longer blur what is behind them, which a phone redrew on every frame.
- The inbox shows the latest message of your three most recently active Paseo sessions, even those you haven't opened since, instead of the last one you saw.
- In a narrow Inbox, such as the one in the left drawer, an item's Mark read, Settle and Snooze show under it as you hover it, instead of over its title, so clicking the title opens it rather than settling it.
- Adding a second Chat in Settings no longer stops the page: each chat keeps its own message box, and sending or dictating focuses the one you used.
- A widget added in Settings, or a pane moved there, is no longer hidden behind Settings: Settings closes to show it, and the added pane is the one focused (on a phone, its panel shows).
- A widget added in Settings while a Changes diff fills the main area shows itself there, instead of the diff, which goes back to its pane.
- A widget liton can't show yet, such as CI runs when a provider offers CI, now says so instead of showing the Capabilities widget.
- Settings' Add a widget marks the widgets already in your layout with a ✓, and screen readers say so. You can still add another, such as a second chat.

### Removed

- **Breaking:** the daemon's `/api` endpoints are gone; use `/liton/v1` (docs/protocol.md).
